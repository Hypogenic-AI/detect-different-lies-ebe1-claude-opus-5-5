# 2607.20479 — Beyond Liars' Bench: The Impact of Lie Typology, Depth, and Sparsity on Deception Detection in LLMs
Amr Moustafa, Max Feser, Florian Mai (University of Bonn / Lamarr Institute). arXiv v1, 29 May 2026. Extended abstract, 9 pp.

## Research question
How do four factors affect white-box deception-probe performance on Liars' Bench: (1) lie typology of training data (fabrication / omission / exaggeration), (2) representation depth (20th vs 66th percentile layer), (3) probe expressivity (linear vs geometric vs polynomial), (4) sparsity (SAE features vs dense hidden states)?

## Definitions / typology
- Lie = on-policy statement contradicting the model's own internal belief (Liars' Bench framing). Explicitly distinguishes deception from **hallucination** (e.g., TruthfulQA) and sycophancy: "previous benchmarks often conflate a model's genuine lack of knowledge with active deception."
- Liars' Bench organizes lies by **reason for lying** (instructed, pressure, harm avoidance, ...) and **object of belief** (world knowledge, self-knowledge, hidden context).
- DolusChat (Cundy & Gleave 2025) types: fabrication, omission, exaggeration.

## Methodology (implementable)
- Follows Liars' Bench protocol (Kretschmar et al. 2025). Disk-backed activation cache.
- **Training**: token-wise activations of response tokens; linear probe trained with **AdamW, BCE loss, lr 1e-3, 20 epochs, batch 64**; training data = Liars'-Bench-style labeled examples + **K = 1000 DolusChat samples**. No contrastive-prompt generation.
- **Evaluation**: hidden states **mean-pooled across the assistant response**; threshold 0.5 for recall.
- **Depths**: 20th percentile (Liars' Bench default) and 66th percentile (motivated by Boxo et al. 2025, arXiv 2508.19505: chance in early layers, peak mid, slight decline late).
- **Probe families**: Logistic regression; Mass-mean (mu_dec - mu_truth, threshold projection); Truth2D (Bürger et al. 2024: 2D truth + polarity subspace); INLP (Ravfogel 2020; iterative null-space projection, as in Boxo et al.); TPC (truncated polynomial classifier, quadratic low-rank interactions; Oldfield et al. ICLR 2026); **Follow-up probe** (append "Was your last response deceptive?" with answer prefilled "No"; LR on final-token activations; Goldowsky-Dill et al. 2025); **Upper-bound probe** (train/test split of follow-up activations within each eval dataset).
- **Sparsity**: Gemma-2-27B layer 31 of 46 (~67%), Gemma Scope `gemma-scope-27b-pt-res-canonical`, width 131,072 (JumpReLU) vs dense.

## Datasets
- Training: DolusChat (~65k examples, roughly balanced fabrication/omission/exaggeration; HF ID not in paper; unverified guess `AlignmentResearch/DolusChat`), sampled 1,000.
- Evaluation (Liars' Bench, arXiv 2511.16035; HF ID not in paper; unverified guess `Cadenza-Labs/liars-bench`): Convincing Game (CG, self-knowledge, instructed), Gender Secret (GS, hidden context, fine-tuned secret), Harm-Pressure Choice (HP-C, WMDP MCQ under harmful context; lie = answer contradicting established belief), Harm-Pressure Knowledge Report (HP-KR, falsely denying knowledge of hazardous task). Alpaca as control.

## Models
Mistral-Small-3.1-24B (depth/expressivity); Gemma-2-27B (dense vs SAE).

## Metrics
AUROC (primary), recall at 0.5. No FPR-constrained metrics, no significance tests.

## Key numbers (AUROC)
- Mistral, Logistic L20/L66: CG .76/.84; GS .40/.56; HP-C .64/**.31**; HP-KR **.14/.13**; avg .47. Truth2D avg .48 (best), TPC .42, Mass-mean .41, INLP .47, Follow-up .41. Upper-bound avg .93 (CG 1.0, GS 1.0, HP-C .75/.82, HP-KR .94/.95).
- Recall (Logistic): CG .91/.99 but GS .08/.10, HP-KR .01/.00 — detectors miss nearly all lies on hidden-context and knowledge-denial lies.
- Gemma-2-27B L31 dense vs SAE (Logistic): CG .67/.67, GS .51/.46, HP-C .48/.48, HP-KR .35/.31; averages dense .53 vs SAE .51 -> SAEs no gain. Upper bound .77.
- Lie typology (Fig. 2, logistic): best training type varies — L20: exaggeration best on CG, no-Dolus on GS, fabrication on HP-C, omission on HP-KR (omission best avg at L20); L66: fabrication best on CG, omission best on HP-C/HP-KR, exaggeration best avg. Full mixture never best.
- **Sub-chance AUROC on HP-KR (0.12-0.16)** = systematic anti-transfer (also reported in Liars' Bench: 0.12 for Llama-3.3-70B mean probe): fabrication-trained direction is *inverted* for "falsely denying knowledge" lies.

## Limitations
Only two depths; no significance testing; typology study only with logistic probe; SAE only at 66%; synthetic DolusChat vs Liars' Bench distribution gap; threshold 0.5 rather than FPR-controlled; extended abstract, sparse detail (e.g., Liars' Bench training split sizes not given).

## Relevance to hallucination vs. lie
- Motivates our study directly (states hallucination benchmarks conflate lack of knowledge with deception) but **does not evaluate hallucinations or honest mistakes**; no false-positive analysis on sincere errors.
- HP-KR (denying knowledge one has) is the inverse of hallucination (claiming knowledge one lacks). The sign-flipped AUROC there suggests probes may be tracking "asserting confident content / claiming knowledge" vs "disclaiming", i.e., a confidence/assertion axis rather than deception per se. This predicts that hallucinations (confident false assertions) could score *as deceptive* under fabrication-trained probes — a concrete hypothesis to test.
- Baselines to borrow: logistic regression (strong, stable), mass-mean, Truth2D, follow-up probe ("Was your last response deceptive?" prefilled "No"), and an in-distribution upper-bound probe to separate "information not present" from "probe doesn't transfer".
- Depth is dataset-dependent: sweep layers rather than fix one; report both ~20% and ~66% depth at minimum.
- Lie type of training data changes detectability non-monotonically — matched questions where only knowledge-state vs incentive differ (our design) avoid this confound.
- Report AUROC plus recall/FPR at fixed thresholds; AUROC < 0.5 should be interpreted as anti-correlation, not noise.
