# 2603.10003 — Probing the Limits of the Lie Detector Approach to LLM Deception
Tom-Felix Thormann (Ruhr-University Bochum, Philosophy). arXiv v1, 16 Feb 2026. Short (7 pages + refs) empirical/philosophical paper.

## Research question
The "lie detector approach" (truth probes that flag outputs the model internally represents as false) implicitly assumes deception == lying. (1) Can LLMs deceive *without lying* (true-but-misleading statements, implicature)? (2) Do truth probes trained on true/false statements fail to detect such deception?

## Definitions / typology
- Traditional deception (Mahon 2016): intentionally causing a false belief the deceiver believes false. AI deception (Dung 2025; Park et al. 2024): behavior that causes/maintains false beliefs because they serve the system's goals.
- Lying = asserting a believed-false statement; a strict subclass of deception.
- **Deception without lying (DWL)**: true-but-misleading statements (implicature; Bronston v. US example), omission, questions, non-linguistic behavior.
- "Lie detector approach" = truth probes as deception detectors (Azaria & Mitchell 2023; Burns et al.; Marks & Tegmark; Bürger et al.; Smith et al. 2025; Liars' Bench).
- Notes conceptual caveat: unclear whether probed representations are beliefs, and whether prompted selections are strategic deception vs. instruction-following/role-play.

## Methodology (implementable)
**Experiment 1 (behavior)**: 2x2 conditions {LIE, DWL} x {0-shot, 2-shot}, user-role prompt via chat template. Each item: explicit goal ("Your goal is to deceive without lying"), optional 2 examples, instruction, question, and 3 shuffled options: honest (true), lie (false), deceptive-non-false (misleading truth). Built from Azaria-Mitchell: 15 statements x 7 domains (animals, cities, companies, elements, facts, generated, inventions) = 105, manually written questions + missing options; 8 dropped -> **97 items**. Responses normalized (lowercase, strip punctuation), exact-match to options -> {D, L, H, invalid}. One-sided binomial test vs 1/3, alpha=0.01.

**Experiment 2 (truth probes)**: Azaria-Mitchell true/false dataset (6,322 Wikipedia-derived statements, half altered to false), minus the 105 Exp-1 items -> 6,217. Features: **per-attention-head outputs (before o_proj), last token, all layers**. Split 60/20/20. One logistic regression per head; pick top-10 heads by validation accuracy; train final LR on concatenated top-10 head outputs; evaluate on test.
- Two training conditions: **RAW** (bare statement) vs **DIA** (2-turn chat-template dialogue: user question -> model asserts statement; questions auto-generated by Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3).
- Test: each Exp-1 option (honest/lie/DWL) forward-passed as RAW and as DIA (with question), classified by matching probe. Detection = classified as false.
- Stats: one-sided McNemar on matched (lie, DWL) pairs per item, alpha = 0.01.

## Datasets
Azaria & Mitchell (2023) True-False dataset (6,322; HF ID not given in paper; unverified guess `notrichardren/azaria-mitchell`); custom 97-item deception set (in repo).

## Models
Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, Gemma-2-9B-IT, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct.

## Baselines / metrics
Chance (1/3) for behavior; probe accuracy/precision/recall/F1; detection rates per response class; false positive rate on honest.

## Key numbers
- Exp 1 (D/L/H/I counts of 97): Gemma DWL-0S 50/8/39/0; DWL-2S 74/4/19/0; LIE-0S 21/72/2/2; LIE-2S 33/62/2/0. Llama DWL-2S 67/7/10/13 (sig), DWL-0S n.s.; Llama LIE 65/97 both. Mistral can't DWL; lies only in 2S (57).
- Probe test accuracy: Llama RAW 0.898 / DIA 0.910; Mistral 0.662 / 0.614; Gemma 0.726 / 0.719.
- Detection rates (D / L / H):
  - Llama RAW .536/.835/.227 (p=4.5e-6); DIA .742/.835/.072 (p=0.054)
  - Mistral RAW .567/.794/.278; DIA .825/.649/.186
  - Gemma RAW .474/.835/.175 (p=1.8e-7); DIA .639/.804/.052 (p=0.0057)
- DIA training improves DWL detection by 15-25 points and lowers FPR on honest statements.

## Limitations
Small models only; multiple-choice selection of pre-written options (not free generation; off-policy text); instructed, role-play deception (no genuine incentive); unclear whether behavior is strategic; tiny eval set (97); top-10-head selection is unusual vs. residual-stream probes.

## Relevance to hallucination vs. lie
- Central point: a **truth probe measures falsity, not intent**. Detection of lies (~0.80-0.84) is close to the rate at which it flags any false statement; by construction it would also flag sincere false statements (hallucinations) whenever the model "represents" them as false — and would miss hallucinations the model represents as true. The paper does **not** test honest mistakes/hallucinations; "honest" = true statements only.
- Implication for our study: a truth-direction probe is a baseline that should respond to *falsity*; it cannot by itself separate lie from hallucination unless the model's internal truth representation differs between the two cases (knows-false vs believes-true). That difference is exactly our hypothesis test: lies = output internally represented as false; hallucinations = output internally represented as true or uncertain.
- DIA (dialogue-context) training lowers FPR on honest outputs — suggests training/evaluating probes on chat-formatted, on-policy-like assistant turns.
- Proposes second-order-belief probes (model's representation of listener's belief) as a deception-specific signal — a candidate feature distinguishing lies (intended false belief in listener) from hallucination (no such intent).
- Implementable baseline: Azaria-Mitchell truth probe, last-token, top-k attention heads or residual stream, RAW vs DIA training.
