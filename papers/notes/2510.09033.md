# 2510.09033 — Do LLMs Really Know What They Don't Know? Internal States Mainly Reflect Knowledge Recall Rather Than Truthfulness
Cheang, Chan, Zhang, Deng (SMU / Alibaba DAMO / SUTD). arXiv v3, Apr 2026.
Code: https://github.com/AndyCheang/knowledge-recall-vs-truthfulness

## Research question
Do internal-state hallucination detectors encode *truthfulness*, or only *whether the model is running a parametric-knowledge-recall process*? Hypothesis: hallucinations driven by spurious-but-strong subject associations are mechanistically like correct recall and thus invisible to probes.

## Definitions (taxonomy)
- **FA (Factual Association)**: greedy output correct.
- **AH (Associated Hallucination)**: wrong, but produced via strong reliance on subject representation (e.g., "Obama was born in Chicago").
- **UH (Unassociated Hallucination)**: wrong, and output weakly depends on subject (model essentially does not know the subject; e.g., obscure "Brenda Johnston ... Portland").
- Note: no "knows-but-misreports" category; UH ≈ "doesn't know", AH ≈ "confidently wrong / thinks it knows".

## Methodology (implementable)
- **Data**: Wikidata triples (s, r, o), relations restricted per Gekhman et al. 2025 "Inside-Out" to person subjects with unique, verifiable objects: P22 father, P25 mother, P26 spouse, P569 date of birth. Cloze templates: "The name of the father of [subject] is", "The name of the mother of [subject] is", "The name of the spouse of [subject] is", "The birth date of [subject] is".
- **Models**: base `meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3-8B`, `mistralai/Mistral-7B-v0.3` (HF), BF16. Refusal tuning via Unsloth QLoRA r=8, α=8, 1 epoch, all attn+MLP modules.
- **Generation**: greedy (T=0), single answer. Knowledge = greedy correctness only (no sampling-based k-of-n).
- **Correctness**: spaCy NER extracts entity; exact match vs gold -> correct; else Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct judge with Gekhman-style prompt (grades A CORRECT / B INCORRECT / C WRONG GOLD / D ERROR; hedges/synonyms/spelling allowed).
- **AH vs UH labeling (causal intervention)**: block attention from all subject tokens to last token (Geva et al. 2023 attention knockout; window of 5 layers when sweeping per layer) and compute Jensen-Shannon divergence between original and blocked next-token distribution. Threshold = mean JS divergence over all FAs. Hallucination with JS > threshold → AH, else UH.
- **Dataset sizes** (12,293 queries each): LLaMA-3-8B FA 3,506 / AH 1,406 / UH 7,381; Mistral FA 3,354 / AH 1,284 / UH 7,655. So ~16% of LLaMA hallucinations are AH.
- **Mechanistic analyses**: (i) per-layer corruption of subject-token and last-token hidden states, attention knockout; (ii) L2 norm of subject representations (avg over subject tokens, ratio vs FA) — UH ≈0.95 of FA at layers 10–15, AH ≈1.0; (iii) subject-to-last attention contribution norm a_last = Σ_k Σ_h A[last,s_k] (h_{s_k} W_V) W_O — AH≈FA high in mid layers, UH low; (iv) last-token cosine similarity within category: FA/AH drop to ≈0.2 by layer 25, UH stays ≈0.5 (UH clustered, template-dominated); t-SNE layer 25; Silhouette UH-vs-FA 0.33–0.40 vs AH-vs-FA ≈0.0; DBI 0.93–1.13 vs 14–25. (v) MLP down-proj subspace overlap ratio lower for UH subjects. (vi) Subject popularity via monthly Wikipedia pageviews (Mallen et al. 2023): low-pop bin 94% UH; high-pop bin FA 52%, AH 14%, UH 34%.
- **Detectors evaluated** (probe = trained classifier on normalized features; 1,000+1,000 train, 200+200 test, 5 seeds, AUROC):
  - Subject representation: last subject token hidden state, 3 consecutive layers (Gottesman & Geva 2024).
  - Attention flow: attention weights from last token to subject tokens across all layers (Yüksekgönül et al. 2024).
  - Last-token representation: final token hidden state at last layer (Orgad et al. 2025).
  - Black-box: answer-token probability; subject popularity.
  - Appendix: semantic consistency (5 samples, T=0.7).

| Method | AH only | UH only |
|---|---|---|
| Subject probe | 0.65 / 0.57 | 0.91 / 0.81 |
| Attention | 0.58 / 0.58 | 0.92 / 0.87 |
| Last token | 0.69 / 0.63 | 0.93 / 0.92 |
| Token probability | 0.49 / 0.46 | 0.86 / 0.89 |
| Subject popularity | 0.48 / 0.52 | 0.87 / 0.84 |
| Semantic consistency (5 samples) | 0.62 / 0.62 | 0.84 / 0.84 |
- Full mixed setting: UH AUROC drops from ~0.9 to ~0.8 when AHs included in training.
- Sensitivity/specificity (last-token, LLaMA): UH sens 0.87/spec 0.93 at t=0.5; AH sens 0.60/spec 0.68.
- Refusal tuning (LLaMA): train on UH → 82% refusal on UH, 28% AH, 29.5% FA over-refusal; train on AH → 33% AH, 23.5% UH.
- Token probability distributions: FA and AH both high-confidence; UH low.

## Limitations
Only short cloze factual completion with 4 relations; base models only; no verbalized-confidence detectors; AH/UH split is defined by an intervention threshold (mean FA JS) that is somewhat arbitrary and partially circular for attention-based analyses (authors acknowledge); single greedy sample for correctness; no deception/lying condition.

## Relevance to our design
- Strong evidence that "hallucination detectors" (probes, logprob, consistency) mostly track *recall-process engagement / confidence*, not truth. Prediction for our study: a **lie** (model knows answer, misreports under incentive) involves full recall of the correct answer, so internal recall signatures should look like FA/AH, not UH. Hallucination detectors trained on correct-vs-wrong would likely score lies as "non-hallucination" (low uncertainty) — this is a testable dissociation, and a key reason detectors might separate lies from hallucinations at all.
- But confidently-wrong AH-type errors (~16% of LLaMA errors here) are a confound: they are "doesn't know" by ground truth but "thinks it knows" internally. Our "knows" definition should be **sample-based consistency of the correct answer**, so AH-type errors (consistent but wrong) get classified as not-knowing-but-confident; we should report detector behavior separately on confident-wrong vs uncertain-wrong hallucinations.
- Implementable detector baselines: last-token final-layer probe, last-subject-token probe, answer token probability, 5-sample consistency at T=0.7.
- Use their LLM-judge prompt for TriviaQA grading (adapt to alias lists), and the popularity covariate if available.
- TriviaQA lacks clean subject spans, so the JS attention-knockout AH/UH split is not directly applicable; a sampling-confidence split is the practical proxy.
