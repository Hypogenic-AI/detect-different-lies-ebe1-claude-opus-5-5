# 2410.02707 — LLMs Know More Than They Show: On the Intrinsic Representation of LLM Hallucinations
Orgad, Toker, Gekhman, Reichart, Szpektor, Kotek, Belinkov (Technion / Google / Apple). ICLR 2025.
Code: https://github.com/technion-cs-nlp/LLMsKnow

## Research question
Where and how do LLM internal states encode the truthfulness of their own free-form generations? Do error-detection probes generalize across tasks? Can error *types* be predicted? Is there a gap between what the model encodes (knows) and what it outputs?

## Definitions
- **Hallucination** = any error by the model (factual, bias, reasoning). Correctness label z ∈ {0,1} vs gold; refusals excluded from detection data.
- **Exact answer tokens**: tokens whose modification changes correctness (e.g., "Hartford" in "The capital of Connecticut is Hartford, ..."). Probed positions: last question token, first/last exact-answer token, token before first / after last, last generated (−1), before last (−2).
- **Error taxonomy from K=30 samples at T=1** (TriviaQA): (A) refuses ≥ half; (B) consistently correct ≥ half — B1 all correct, B2 mostly; (C) consistently incorrect (same wrong answer ≥ half) — C1 correct never produced, C2 correct appears ≥1; (D) two competing (correct & incorrect at similar rates, difference ≤5, each ≥5 times); (E) many answers (>10 distinct) — E1 correct never, E2 correct appears. Non-orthogonal; covers 96% of Mistral-7B-Instruct TriviaQA errors. K=30 chosen because "at least one correct" coverage plateaus (~0.69 greedy → ~0.865 at 31 tries, Fig. 10). Lower temperatures gave less truthful answers across trials.

## Methodology (implementable)
- **Models**: Mistral-7B(v0.1?), Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2, Llama-3-8B, Llama-3-8B-Instruct.
- **Datasets** (10K train / 10K test unless smaller): TriviaQA (no context, alias list), HotpotQA ± context, Natural Questions with context, Movies ("Who acted as [figure] in the movie [movie]?", from Kaggle The Movies Dataset), Winogrande, Winobias (~1.5K/split), MNLI, Math (Sun et al. 2024 answerable; ~2K/650), IMDB (1-shot). Prompts: question with no/short instruction; free-form greedy generation (exact formats in repo).
- **Correctness**: heuristic — correct if output contains a gold alias and it appears before any alternative incorrect label; LLM judge only for NQ-WC (prompt in App. A.2: "Evaluate the following answers... write '1' if correct, '0' if not", few-shot).
- **Exact answer extraction**: if gold alias found in output, locate it; else Mistral-7B-Instruct few-shot extractor ("Extract from the following long answer the short answer, only the relevant tokens. If the long answer does not answer the question, output NO ANSWER."), up to 5 tries, must be substring; questions without valid extraction dropped. Self-extraction success 0.95–0.99.
- **Probe**: sklearn LogisticRegression (L2, lbfgs, defaults) on the **output of the final MLP** at layer l (residual / attention gave similar results), at a chosen token; layer/token chosen on validation (80/20 split, test bootstrapped). Best: last exact answer token, middle-to-late layers.
- **Baselines**: Logits-mean/min/max and Probas-mean/min/max over whole answer or exact tokens; **P(True)** (Kadavath 2022) prompt: "Question: [Q]\nProposed Answer: [A]\nIs the proposed answer:\n(A) True\n(B) False\nThe proposed answer is:".
- **Generalization**: train on one dataset, test on others; report max(AUC, 1−AUC) and difference vs Logit-min-exact.
- **Error-type probes**: one-vs-rest probes on greedy-answer representations.
- **Answer selection**: among 30 samples, choose the one with highest probe correctness probability; compare to greedy, random, majority vote.

## Key numbers
- Error detection AUC (Mistral-7B-Instruct / Llama-3-8B-Instruct, TriviaQA): Logits-mean 0.60/0.66; Logits-min-exact 0.75/0.79; P(True) 0.66/0.73; P(True)-exact 0.74/0.73; probe last token 0.71/0.81; probe end-of-question 0.76/0.77; **probe exact answer 0.85/0.83**. Mistral-7B base TriviaQA: Probas-min-exact 0.85, probe exact 0.84, end-of-question 0.74.
- Cross-task generalization mostly explained by logit-based confidence; real transfer only within similar skills (TriviaQA ↔ HotpotQA ↔ Movies; Winobias ↔ Winogrande ↔ NLI). "Truthfulness" is skill-specific, not universal.
- Error-type AUC (TriviaQA; Mistral / Mistral-Instr / Llama / Llama-Instr): refuses 0.85–0.88; consistently correct 0.81–0.88; **consistently incorrect 0.59–0.67**; two competing 0.61–0.68; many answers 0.84–0.90. → consistent (confident) errors are hardest to separate internally.
- Answer selection, TriviaQA Mistral-7B-Instruct accuracy: greedy 0.63, random 0.64, majority 0.67, probe 0.71; for C2 (mostly incorrect, correct appears) greedy 0.11 → probe 0.53; D 0.32 → 0.78; E2 0.23 → 0.56. Llama-3-8B-Instruct: all 0.69 → 0.73; C2 0.12 → 0.43. "Model encodes correct answer yet consistently generates a wrong one."
- Pre-generation (end-of-question) probe already gives ~0.72–0.77 AUC on TriviaQA → much of "hallucination detection" is predicting whether the model knows the question.

## Limitations
Open models only (7–8B); QA with gold labels; probes trained per task/model; exact-answer extraction needs an external LLM; taxonomy non-orthogonal; no deception condition; "knowledge" of correct answer inferred from probe selection, not causally established.

## Relevance to our design
- **Knowledge operationalization**: the K=30, T=1 sample distribution gives a graded knowledge profile; type B1/B2 (correct ≥ 50% of samples) = model "knows"; C1/E1 (correct never appears in 30) = does not know; C2/D/E2 = partial/hidden knowledge (exclude or analyze separately). Combine with WACK's stricter "greedy + all samples correct".
- **Hallucination detectors to implement** for our lie-vs-hallucination test: (1) probe on last exact-answer token (MLP output, mid-late layer, logistic regression) trained on honest correct vs honest incorrect; (2) end-of-question probe (pre-generation "do I know this?"); (3) Logits-min-exact / Probas-min-exact; (4) P(True) with the Kadavath prompt; (5) sample-based consistency (majority frequency / number of distinct answers) as a semantic-entropy proxy. Key prediction: under a lie incentive on known questions, the end-of-question "knows" probe should say "known", while answer-token probes trained on correctness may flag the false answer — the dissociation is our test.
- Important confound: answer-token probes partly track output token probability; a lie may be produced with high or low token probability depending on prompt — report logit baselines alongside probes and check whether probe adds beyond logits (their Fig. 3b logic).
- Use their correctness heuristic (gold alias appears first) and exact-answer extraction pipeline for TriviaQA; their repo has prompts and splits.
- Cross-task non-generalization warns that a lie probe trained on one lie-inducing prompt family may not transfer; evaluate across prompt families.
